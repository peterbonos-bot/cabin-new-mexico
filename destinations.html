Village, culture & contemplation

Begin close to home.

01

Hot springs & village life

Jemez Springs

The village brings together mineral hot springs, local shops, and places to stop for food. Plan a soak at a bathhouse or spa, or explore the nearby Jemez Historic Site. Sky Cabin is approximately 10 minutes away by car.

02

Jemez Pueblo visitor center

Walatowa Visitor Center

Start your visit to Jemez Pueblo here. The Walatowa History Museum introduces Hemish history and living Towa traditions, while the gift shop features work by Indigenous artists. The center also provides Red Rocks Trails permits and visitor guidance.

03

The Bodhi · Buddhist practice & retreat

Bodhi Manda Zen Center

A Rinzai Zen Buddhist center in Jemez Springs, founded in 1973. Beginners and experienced practitioners can learn about meditation, quiet contemplation, and retreats. Check the visiting page and contact the center about current programs and arrangements.

Canyons, rivers & wide-open country

Follow the landscape.

04

Railroad history & canyon scenery

Gilman Tunnels

Two former railroad tunnels cut through ancient rock above the Rio Guadalupe. The dramatic canyon setting makes this a memorable scenic outing. Check Forest Service road conditions before setting out; access can change with seasonal conditions or maintenance.

Learn about the tunnels ↗Forest alerts & access ↗
05

Water through the mountains

Jemez River

The East Fork and San Antonio Creek meet at Battleship Rock to form the Jemez River. The river corridor is a beautiful part of exploring the region, with fishing and recreation opportunities at designated public sites. Check access rules and current fishing requirements before your visit.

Learn about the river ↗Outdoor visitor guide ↗
06

Red Rocks Trails · Jemez Pueblo

Walatowa Slot Canyon

Explore red sandstone, a narrow slot canyon, and mountain views on the Red Rocks Trails. The self-guided route is about 1.5 miles. A hiking permit is required from the Walatowa Visitor Center; check current hours, conditions, and guided options with the center.

Trail details & permits ↗
07

Volcanic valleys & mountain trails

Valles Caldera National Preserve

A broad volcanic landscape of grasslands, forest, and open views in the Jemez Mountains. Plan hiking, wildlife watching, or a scenic visit using the National Park Service guide, which explains seasonal access and any reservations needed for your chosen activity.

Plan your preserve visit ↗
08

Rock formations · A regional day trip

Kasha-Katuwe Tent Rocks

Distinctive cone-shaped rock formations and canyon trails reward a carefully planned visit. Entry requires both a BLM ticket reservation and a separate Cochiti Pueblo Tribal Access Pass. Review the official check-in process, seasonal availability, and access requirements before traveling.

The gateway town

Bernalillo & the Rio Grande.

09

Your nearby city stop

Bernalillo

A useful stop on the way between the Jemez region and the Albuquerque area. Explore Coronado Historic Site and the remains of Kuaua Pueblo beside the Rio Grande, then continue toward the mountains along the Jemez Mountain Trail route.
